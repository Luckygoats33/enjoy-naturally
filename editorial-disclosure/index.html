Disclosure

Editorial disclosure

Readers should be able to tell who publishes Enjoy Naturally, how a story is sourced, and when a business interest is in the room.

Ownership

Enjoy Naturally is my own blog about recipes, the kitchen, the garden, the home, and everyday life. The about page says what I write about and what I will not claim.

Independence, stated honestly

I write for people first: to explain a process, share a recipe, or make a season easier to live in. A product mention is rare. When one happens, I say so in the story, and it is not a health claim.

Sources

Factual statements about food processing, labels, nutrition data, and food safety cite a public source. We prefer U.S. government pages, such as FDA, USDA, and CDC, and university extension publications. A source is linked from the story. If a number is uncertain, we leave the number out rather than guess.

Health language

We do not claim that a food or herb treats, cures, prevents, or manages a disease or condition. We do not claim it boosts immunity or detoxifies the body. The medical disclaimer explains the limit of what a lifestyle journal can say.

Affiliates and sponsors

This preview has no affiliate links and no sponsored stories. If either appears later, the story or link will say so in plain language before a reader is asked to click.

Corrections

If we publish an error, we will correct the story and note the change when the error is more than a typo. Send corrections through the contact page.

Editing tools

Editors may use software to help draft a story. A person reviews every story before it is published. Software does not decide what Enjoy Naturally stands behind.

Photographs

Images in this first version were created for the journal. They do not document a real reader's home. Captions on article pages say so. Future photographs of people will be used with permission and credit.

A personal blog, said plainly

Enjoy Naturally is written in the first person because it is one person's blog. Recipes, yoga, and the rest of ordinary life. I am not a secret review desk for a company, and I am not going to invent a parent brand in the footer. If that ever changed, this page would change in the same week, in plain language, above the fold of the disclosure.

What counts as a fact here

When I describe how freeze-drying works, what a lunchbox temperature rule is, or how a label uses a word, I am making a factual claim. Those get a link to a public source I can stand behind: a government page, a university extension, or a document that says the thing I said. When I describe what I like, that is taste. Taste does not get disguised as a study.

I would rather cut a number than guess one. If I am unsure, the sentence does not ship.

Products, ads, and the word "partner"

This blog is not an affiliate machine. If I use a product, I say I used it. If someone pays me, or gives me something, to mention them, the story will say that before you are asked to care. A mention is never permission to imply the thing treats, cures, or prevents a condition. I will not take that kind of partnership.

Corrections and tools

If a factual error is more than a typo, I correct the story and note the change. Write me through the contact page. I may use software to help draft. I still decide what I am willing to publish. Software does not get a byline, and it does not get to invent a source.
