Creamy Wild Rice & Mushroom Soup

November 5, 2026 · 2 min read · Food & Recipes

Dinner for 4 in about an hour. Most of that is simmering. Start it, then do something else.

Serves 4About 1 hourMostly hands-off simmering

Ingredients

  • 1 cup wild rice blend, or ¾ cup wild rice plus ¼ cup brown rice, rinsed
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 12 ounces mixed mushrooms, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon fresh thyme leaves, or ½ teaspoon dried thyme
  • 6 cups unsalted vegetable broth, plus more if the pot looks dry
  • ½ cup milk, or unsweetened oat milk
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Chopped parsley, for the bowls
  • Crusty bread, for serving

Method

  1. Warm the oil in a heavy pot over medium heat. Cook the onion with a pinch of salt until it turns soft and clear, about 6 to 8 minutes.
  2. Add the garlic, mushrooms, and thyme. Cook until the mushrooms release their liquid and the edges brown, about 8 minutes. Do not rush this part. The browning is the flavor.
  3. Stir in the rice so the grains pick up the oil, then pour in the broth. Bring the pot to a simmer.
  4. Cover and cook until the rice is tender. Wild rice is ready when many of the dark grains have split open. A blend often needs 40 to 50 minutes. Check the time on your package, and add a splash of water if the surface looks dry before the rice is done.
  5. Stir in the milk and the lemon juice. Taste. Add salt and pepper a little at a time. If the broth was already salted, you may need very little.
  6. Ladle into warm bowls, finish with parsley, and serve with bread.

Notes from the pot

  • For a thicker soup, scoop out about a cup, blend it until smooth, and stir it back in.
  • Skip the milk and finish with a spoon of olive oil if you want the soup fully free of dairy.
  • Cool leftovers in a shallow container and refrigerate them within 2 hours. USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service explains why on its Danger Zone page: perishable food should not sit between 40°F and 140°F. Reheat until the soup is steaming.

The night I make it

This is a Sunday pot or a Wednesday pot, depending on whether I planned. I start it when I get home and I do other small things while the rice thinks about being tender. Answer a text. Roll out the mat for eight minutes. Wipe the table. The soup does not need me to stare at it, which is why it is my November dinner.

I eat it in a bowl with bread if I have bread, and with parsley if the bunch has not fainted. The second night is often better. I cool leftovers in a shallow container, not in the pot, and I reheat until the bowl is steaming. That is the only safety speech this recipe needs, and the source is linked above if you want the agency's words instead of mine.

How I change it without ruining it

  • No milk in the house. A spoon of olive oil at the end. Still cozy. Less "creamy," more "pot of mushrooms."
  • More vegetables. A handful of spinach in the last five minutes, or celery with the onion if I am trying to use the drawer.
  • Thicker. Blend a cup and stir it back. I do this when I want it to feel like a meal and not a brothy evening.
  • Herb swap. Thyme is the one I trust. Rosemary is louder. Use less if you switch.

If you make it, you do not owe me a report. If the rice is still stubborn at the time on the package, give it more water and more patience. Wild rice is done when a lot of the dark grains split. That is a better test than the clock.

The rest of an autumn table

I put the soup out with bread, a pile of roasted squash if the oven is already on, and apple slices for anyone who wants something sweet after. Thyme from the windowsill if the plant is alive. A walk first if the day was stale, then this bowl. It is the dinner I make when I want the evening to feel like fall without decorating anything.
