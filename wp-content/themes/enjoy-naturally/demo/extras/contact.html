What to write me about

I read notes from readers. The useful ones are specific. A correction with the sentence you think is wrong. A recipe you made and what your stove did. A walk you took because a post made the block sound worth it. A question about yoga that is really a question about a sequence, not about a diagnosis.

  • Corrections. Tell me the page and the claim. If I had a source and it does not say what I said, I want to know.
  • Story ideas. Something you cook, a pose you return to, a season where you live. I cannot promise I will write it. I can promise I will not pretend I invented your life.
  • Partnerships. I rarely say yes. If you ask, say what the product is in one sentence and do not ask me to imply it treats anything.
  • Something else. Kindness helps. So does brevity.

What not to send

Do not send medical details, photos of a child's body, passwords, or anything you would not want stored on a website. I am not a clinic. I cannot tell you whether a food or a stretch is safe for your body. That conversation belongs with a licensed clinician. The medical disclaimer is blunt about this on purpose.

On this preview, the form keeps the message in your browser so you can see the box work. It is not delivered to me. On the WordPress site, the same note is saved for me and the site tries to email it. Either way, do not use it as urgent care. If you need a clinician today, go there today.
