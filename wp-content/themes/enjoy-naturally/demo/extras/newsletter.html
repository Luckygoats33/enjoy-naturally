What a letter actually contains

When I send one, it is short enough to read while something simmers. One recipe I cooked. One yoga or walking note that fit the week. One small thing from the house or the street. I write it the way I talk. If I did not do the thing, it does not go in the letter.

A November letter might be the wild rice soup, eight minutes on the mat, and the fact that I finally put gloves by the door. A July letter would not pretend to be November. That is the whole editorial policy of the inbox.

How often, and how you leave

Not daily. Not a drip that trains you to ignore me. I would rather skip a week than send filler. When a real mailing list is connected, I expect a confirmation email before you are subscribed, and a way to leave that does not require a scavenger hunt. I will not sell the list. I will not add your address because you once liked a post.

Until a mailing list is connected, saving an address on the WordPress site does not subscribe you and does not email you. If a form says it only stores an address in the browser, believe it.

What you will not get

No discount code dressed up as friendship. No "detox with me." No email that tells you a snack treats a condition. If I ever mention something I bought, it will be because I used it, and it will not come with a health claim. You can also just read the seasonal page and never subscribe. The blog works without the inbox.
