Herbs as flavor, not a prescription

I grow mint because I like it in a glass of water. I cook with thyme because mushrooms taste more like themselves. Chamomile is a cup I drink when I want something warm that is not coffee. That is the whole standard for this section. If I cannot tell you how it tastes or how it grows, I am not ready to publish it.

Traditional uses can be part of a story. My grandmother's tea is a family story. It is not evidence that the tea treats an illness. I will not write that an herb cures, prevents, or manages a condition. I will not tell you to swap it for a medicine you already take. If you are pregnant, nursing, giving herbs to a child, or on medication, ask a clinician before you use herbs as anything other than ordinary food.

What is usually on my sill

  • Mint. A pot that wants water and a trim. I tear leaves into drinks and fruit salads.
  • Thyme. The one I trust in the soup pot. It dries well if I actually remember to hang a bunch.
  • Parsley. For finishing a bowl so dinner looks like I cared.
  • Chives, when I can keep them. Scissors, a baked potato, done.

A windowsill counts. You do not need a bed. If the plant dies, I have killed plenty. Buy another and water it before it becomes a moral lesson.

How I write about a cup of tea

I say what it smells like, when I drink it, and whether I grow it or buy it. If I mention a tradition, I name it as tradition. If I mention a study, I link it and I do not stretch one paper into a promise. The medical disclaimer is the longer version of this boundary. Read it before you treat a blog as care.

The kitchen pieces that already use herbs are the mushroom soup and the everyday cooking notes on the recipe desk. A beginner's guide to cooking with fresh herbs is on my list. It will be about knives, stems, and taste. It will not be a protocol.

Herbs I use more when it is cold

Thyme goes into the mushroom soup and the roasted squash. Sage is one or two leaves in browned butter if I am feeling fancy, not a handful. Mint from the last cutting steeps in hot water because I like the smell, not because it treats a cold. I say that every time on purpose.

If a frost is coming, I cut what I can use in a week and dry the rest in a loose bundle, away from the stove's steam. A windowsill pot of parsley still earns its place in November. I snip it onto bowls so dinner looks finished.
