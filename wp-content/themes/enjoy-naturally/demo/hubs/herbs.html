Five herbs, and exactly where each one goes in a November pot or cup.

Five herbs I cook with

  1. Thyme. One teaspoon fresh leaves, or 1/2 teaspoon dried, in mushroom soup or on roasted squash. Add it while the vegetables are cooking, not at the table.
  2. Parsley. A small handful, chopped, on the bowl at the end. It is there for freshness. Stir it in off the heat.
  3. Sage. One or two leaves, fried in a teaspoon of butter until crisp, on roasted squash. More than that tastes like soap.
  4. Rosemary. One small sprig in a pot of beans or soup, pulled out before you serve. It is loud. Do not leave the needles in the bowl.
  5. Mint. A few leaves torn into hot water for a cup, or into sliced apples. Grow it in a pot so it does not take the bed.

One cup and one pot

Mint cup, 5 minutes. Tear 6 to 8 mint leaves into a mug. Pour over hot water. Steep 5 minutes. Drink it because you like the taste. This is not a remedy.

Thyme in the soup. Add 1 teaspoon fresh thyme (or 1/2 teaspoon dried) when the mushrooms go into the wild rice soup. It cooks with them for the whole simmer. Finish with parsley, not more thyme.

If you are pregnant, nursing, giving herbs to a child, or taking medication, keep herbs at ordinary food amounts and ask a clinician before you use them any other way. More is on the medical disclaimer.
