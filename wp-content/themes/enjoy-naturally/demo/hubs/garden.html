One hour outside. The beds are covered, the dead vines are gone, and you are back indoors.

Put the beds to bed

Do this once before a hard freeze. You need gloves, a bucket, and whatever leaves are already on the ground.

  1. Walk the bed and pull plants that are finished: black tomatoes, collapsed vines, anything slimy. Cut them at the soil. Compost healthy debris. Bag leaves or fruit that are spotted or moldy so you do not spread that in the pile.
  2. Leave a few standing seed heads if birds are still feeding. Sunflowers and coneflowers can stay. Everything else that is done comes out.
  3. Weed the open soil while you can still see it. Ten minutes here saves a spring mess.
  4. Shred fallen leaves if you can (a mower, shears, or your hands). Whole wet mats smother crowns.
  5. Spread the leaves 2 to 3 inches deep on bare soil. Keep them off woody trunks and off the crown of any plant that is still alive.
  6. Empty pots you will not water. Store them so they do not fill with ice. Move a pot of parsley or a hardy green to the window you actually look at.
  7. Coil the hose and shut off the outdoor faucet if a freeze is in your forecast. That step is local. If you are unsure, check your water utility or extension office.
  8. Write five crops you want in spring on a piece of paper and leave it in the kitchen. Then go inside.

If you only have a windowsill, do steps 6 and 8. A garden can be one living pot.
