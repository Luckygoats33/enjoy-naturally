Garden

What I'm doing in the pots and beds, written down so I don't forget it by spring. Chores and small pleasures, not a harvest contest.

I am a home grower, not a farm

My garden is pots, a couple of beds, and the leaves that land on them. I write this section the way I text a friend: what I did on Saturday, what failed, and what I want to remember in March. There is no yield contest. If the kale is small, the kale is small.

Late autumn is when I stop pretending summer is still happening. Vines that are done come out. A few seed heads stay for the birds. Bare soil gets a blanket. I would rather do a short, slightly messy job now than stare at a frozen mess in January and call it a plan.

What I actually do in November

  • Clear what is finished. Spent vines, slimy tomatoes, anything that is mold pretending to be a plant. I leave a few standing stalks if they still feed birds.
  • Cover bare soil. Leaves, if I can shred them, or a layer I already have. I do not pile wet mats against a trunk.
  • Garlic, if my winter allows it. I do not pretend one national date works for every yard. I follow the climate I live in and a local extension note if I am unsure.
  • One pot of hardy greens. Something I can cut from the kitchen window when I do not want to put on a coat.
  • A paper plan. A page that says what I want in spring, so January is not a blank stare.

Leaves are not a mess I have to defeat

I used to bag every leaf like the yard had failed a test. Now I treat them as the blanket the soil was going to get anyway. Shredded leaves disappear into the bed. Whole wet leaves can mat and smother, so I break them up if I can. I keep them off the crown of a plant and off the bark of a tree. That is the whole method. The pretty name is leaf mulch. The real name is "do not throw away the free blanket."

What is still in the notebook

A full checklist for putting the beds to sleep, a climate-honest note on what to plant in November, and a how-to on mulch depth are still drafts. I would rather publish them when the steps match what I did with my own hands. Until then, the list above is the version I am willing to stand behind.

If you are in an apartment, ignore the beds and keep the windowsill pot and the paper plan. A garden can be one living thing you remember to water.

Autumn chores I will actually finish

Fall in the garden is a closing shift, not a new hobby. I give myself one hour. If the hour ends, I stop, even if a corner is still messy. The beds survive my honesty better than they survive a heroic Saturday I resent.

  • Cut finished vines at the soil and compost what is not diseased.
  • Leave sunflowers or coneflowers standing if birds are still using them.
  • Lay shredded leaves two or three inches deep on bare soil. Keep them off woody trunks.
  • Pot up parsley or a hardy green and put it where I see it when I boil water.
  • Write five crops I want in spring on paper, not in an app I will forget.

The first frost is my reminder to cut mint and hang it for tea. I like the smell in the kitchen. I am not drying it as a remedy. If your frost date is different from mine, trust your own week, not a national calendar.
