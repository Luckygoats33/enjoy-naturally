Food & Recipes

Recipes I cook on weeknights, plus the label notes I wish someone had told me plainly. Practical kitchen stuff. Not medical advice.

What cooking looks like at my place

I cook because I get hungry, not because I am building a brand. Most nights it is one pan, a bowl of something warm, and whatever fruit or greens I actually bought. This is the kitchen part of the blog: recipes I have made more than once, notes on how a food is made, and the label questions I got tired of guessing about.

I like food I can picture. Squash in a pot. Apples under oats. Yogurt with whatever berries are in the freezer. If a recipe needs a specialty pan I do not own, I say so and give the bowl I used instead. Times are the times from my stove. Yours may run hotter. Taste as you go.

A week of eating, without a performance

Monday is often eggs or leftover soup. Tuesday might be a tray of vegetables and something that was already cooked. Wednesday I want a real recipe, which lately means the wild rice soup or a crisp if I have apples. I do not meal-prep a grid of identical boxes. I write down the four dinners that survive a tired week, and I repeat them.

  • Breakfast I will actually eat. Oats, yogurt, or toast with fruit. If I sweeten it, I can taste that I did.
  • Lunch that is not a project. Last night's pot, a piece of cheese, cucumbers, an apple.
  • Dinner that waits. Soup, a tray bake, or rice with whatever vegetable is softest in the drawer.
  • A snack with a size. I pour a portion. Eating from the bag is how I lose track, especially with dried fruit.

How I read a package

The front of a bag is an advertisement. The ingredient list is the recipe, written by weight. I look there first. Short is not always better, and long is not always a problem, but I want to recognize most of the words. If sugar shows up in the first few ingredients of something sold as fruit, I am looking at candy that learned a new vocabulary.

The word "natural" on a box does not mean organic, unprocessed, or better for me. I treat it as a marketing word with a narrow history, and I still read the back. When I write about a process, like freeze-drying, I link a public source. I am explaining what happens to the food. I am not promising what it will do to a body.

Recipes you can make from here

The soup, the snacking notes, and the freeze-dried fruit explainer are the pieces that are finished. The apple oat crisp is still in my notebook because I want the pan size and the time to be honest before I publish them. If you only make one thing, make the creamy wild rice and mushroom soup. It is the pot I trust on a cold night.

Nothing in this kitchen is medical advice, a detox, or a cure. If you are changing how you eat because a clinician told you to, follow that person, not a blog.

Fall recipes I keep coming back to

When the light goes earlier, I stop making salads as dinner and start making bowls that stay warm. These are the autumn pots and trays from my own kitchen. Amounts are what I used. Taste yours.

Sheet-pan squash

A small butternut or a couple of delicata, cut, olive oil, salt, and thyme. Roast until the edges brown. I eat it over warm grains or next to an egg. If I have yogurt, a spoon on top. That is dinner on a Wednesday.

Cabbage and apple skillet

Shredded cabbage, one apple in slices, a little butter or oil, salt. Cook until the cabbage is sweet at the edges. I eat it beside sausage if I have it, or with toasted bread if I do not. It uses the apple that was getting soft.

Cinnamon oats before the mat

Oats, milk or water, a pinch of cinnamon, fruit on top. I eat this, then I get on the floor for ten minutes while the apartment is still quiet. The recipe is not the point. The order is: warm food, then movement, then the day.

Baked apples

Cores scooped, a little maple and oats in the middle, baked until they slump. I make two. One is dessert. One is breakfast if it survives. The crisp in the notebook is the dressed-up version of this, and I will publish the times when I have baked it enough times to trust them.

The finished autumn pot is still the wild rice and mushroom soup. If you make only one thing from this page in November, make that.
